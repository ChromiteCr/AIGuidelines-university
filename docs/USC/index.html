[S1-Q1] USC short-answer context

Your USC short-answer questions help us get to know your academic interests and how you might fit into our campus culture.

[S1-Q2] AI authorship discouraged

You don’t need a dramatic life story. You just need to speak sincerely. If you’re funny, be funny. If you’re serious, that’s okay too. The best essays sound like they were written by the real you... not by someone trying to impress a committee with big words and vague ideas, not by adults, and definitely not by AI.

[S1-Q3] own words

So, take a deep breath. Write in your own words.

[S2-Q1] own voice

Use your own words and way of speaking when writing your essay. Write how you typically would to a friend, parent, classmate, teacher, etc. Be sure to be professional but stay true to your personal writing style.

[S2-Q2] human feedback

Please have someone else look at your essay! You don’t need a million different opinions but getting input from one or two other people will help you see your essay from a different perspective. Make sure you pick someone you’re comfortable receiving feedback from, whether that be a teacher, friend, counselor, etc.

[S2-Q3] proofreading advice

Make sure to triple check your essay for punctuation and grammatical errors.

AI detection

USC states that detectors cannot definitively identify AI writing and may produce false positives. High-school adaptation takeaways

  • A school-provided AI account is not blanket permission for assignments.
  • Define uncredited AI-generated and AI-paraphrased work as misrepresentation.
  • Require factual and citation checks plus an AI-use disclosure.
  • Ban unauthorized entry of student records, health data, private communications, and class materials.
  • Never discipline from a detector percentage alone; review drafts, sources, process, and explanation.
