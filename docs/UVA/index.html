[S1-Q1] limited AI assistance and no copy-paste

Generative AI may play a minor role in your final product by helping you brainstorm essay topics or check for grammar mistakes, but we ask that you do not cut and paste any part of your essay or personal statements from an AI tool. All final submitted materials should be your original work.

[S1-Q2] honor statement and authorship

As an applicant to UVA, we ask you to sign an honor statement before submitting the Common Application because the UVA community is anchored by trust and integrity. Our students pledge not to lie, cheat, or steal, and we ask the same of our applicants. By agreeing to this honor statement, you are pledging that the application materials you submit to us are your original work, not primarily a product of AI.

AI can produce biased, misleading, or false claims, misattribute content, and invent sources.摘录Excerpt Students must critically assess results, verify factual claims through reliable sources, and remain responsible for submitted work.核实责任Verification duty

Privacy and intellectual property

Students should not enter sensitive, personal, or proprietary information into AI systems. External services may retain account data, prompts, and conversations or use them for training.数据与隐私Data & privacy Instructors should not require accounts that demand personal information; UVA recommends protected institutional tools when appropriate.机构工具Institutional tools

UVA states that students’ original work is, in most cases, their intellectual property. Instructors should not upload it to services that add submissions to training data, and an assignment requiring such an upload must offer an alternative.课程规则优先Instructor override知识产权IP & copyright

AI detection

UVA discourages AI detectors for academic-fraud decisions because reliability has not been demonstrated. Student work must not be uploaded to a detector that adds it to a database, and detector output should not be treated as proof.检测器证据Detector evidence

High-school adaptation takeaways

  • Require teachers to publish assignment-specific AI permissions.
  • Disclose meaningful assistance, including brainstorming and editing, not only copied prose.
  • Verify claims and citations with textbooks, library databases, or other reliable sources.
  • Offer a non-AI alternative when an activity requires account creation or upload of original work.
  • Use detector results, if at all, only to prompt process review and a conversation—not as proof.
