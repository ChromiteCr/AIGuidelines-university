## Appropriate Use of Generative AI Tools

- Publishing unit: Office of Ethics, Risk, and Compliance Services (OERCS)
- Official source: https://oercs.berkeley.edu/appropriate-use-generative-ai-tools

---

The advisory supports innovation while protecting institutional, personal, and proprietary information.

### Prohibited use

- “Completion of academic work in a manner not allowed by the instructor.”
- Unless specifically allowed, “no personal, confidential, proprietary, or otherwise sensitive information may be entered into or generated as output.”
- Protected examples include FERPA student records, non-public instructional materials, proprietary or unpublished research, and information classified at higher protection levels.

If considering a new use of generative AI in studies or work, the user is responsible for considering the ethics and risks and must “obtain approval from your instructor/responsible unit head.”

UC legal guidance quoted by Berkeley warns that, unless specific precautions are taken, “any content that is provided to generative AI tools can be saved and reused by the company offering the tool.”

## Course policy and acknowledgment

- Publishing unit: Center for Teaching and Learning
- Additional official source: https://teaching.berkeley.edu/teaching-strategies/ai-teaching-learning/communicating-course-policies-and-talking-students

Clear course policies should explain what is allowed and why, connect AI rules to learning goals, and support transparency, integrity, and shared responsibility. When AI is permitted, instructors may require students to include an acknowledgment and share the prompts and outputs used at submission.

The guidance states that GenAI should never be used in a way that would constitute plagiarism if the source were human. Having another person or entity write a substantive part of an assignment can include using GenAI tools.

The page also states that GenAI detection tools are not a viable or recommended enforcement option. Berkeley does not enable Turnitin’s AI detector because of privacy, security, accessibility, effectiveness, and actionability concerns.

## High-school adaptation takeaways

- Teacher permission overrides general availability of a tool.
- Never enter student records, unpublished work, private teaching materials, or other sensitive data into unapproved AI systems.
- Require a short acknowledgment and, where useful, the prompts and relevant outputs.
- Do not use AI detectors as automatic proof of misconduct.
