## Guidelines on Using Generative Artificial Intelligence for Coursework

- Publishing unit: Office of the Provost
- Official source: https://policies.dartmouth.edu/policy/guidelines-using-generative-artificial-intelligence-genai-coursework

---

Dartmouth recognizes GenAI as a powerful learning tool but gives instructors authority to decide whether it may be used, based on course and assignment learning outcomes.

### Default rule

> **Students may not use GenAI tools for submitted coursework unless expressly permitted.**

Dartmouth therefore treats GenAI as disallowed by default, while allowing instructors to authorize it where pedagogically appropriate.

### Course-specific policy

Instructors are encouraged to define AI policies in the syllabus. For academic integrity in a course, the instructor’s GenAI policy defines the expectations for faculty, students, and staff.

### Acknowledgment

Whenever GenAI is permitted, its use remains subject to the Academic Honor Policy. Students must confirm the required disclosure or acknowledgment method with the instructor. If no specific method is provided, students must use a discipline-appropriate citation or acknowledgment. Failure to acknowledge GenAI use may lead to disciplinary action.

### Duty to clarify

“It is the student's responsibility to clarify questions with their instructor regarding GenAI use in a course and/or instructor expectations for acknowledgement of GenAI.” Failure to clarify expectations is not an acceptable justification for violating the Academic Honor Policy.

The policy provides model acknowledgments that identify the tool, model/version, date, prompt or task, and the student’s review or revision.

## High-school adaptation takeaways

- A clear default-prohibited-unless-permitted rule is easy for younger students to understand.
- Every permitted use still requires acknowledgment.
- A disclosure can identify the tool, date, prompt, task, and what the student reviewed or changed.
- “I did not know” should not replace the duty to ask before submitting.
