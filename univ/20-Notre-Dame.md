## Generative AI Policy for Students

- Publishing unit: Undergraduate Academic Code of Honor
- Official source: https://honorcode.nd.edu/generative-ai-policy-for-students-august-2023

---

Notre Dame recognizes that GenAI can support education by helping students think through ideas, make study guides or practice problems, and understand difficult concepts.

However, using AI “as a substitute for genuine engagement, or to circumvent the necessary intellectual challenges of your coursework, runs counter to the heart of education itself.” Students should distinguish between supplementing education and replacing it.

The policy states:

> “Representing work that you did not produce as your own, including work generated or materially modified by AI, constitutes academic dishonesty.”

Using GenAI contrary to an instructor’s stated policy, or using it to complete coursework in a way not expressly permitted by the instructor, violates the Honor Code.

## University AI principles

- Additional official source: https://ai.nd.edu/ai-in-action/policies-and-guidelines

Notre Dame’s broader guidance says AI-generated content may be biased, inaccurate, fabricated, or contain copyrighted or proprietary information, so thorough human review is required before use. AI use in university research, scholarship, or work should be clearly documented and disclosed.

A later policy clarification notes that AI editing tools such as Grammarly and WordTune also generate text. If an instructor prohibits GenAI, that prohibition includes such editing tools unless explicitly stated otherwise.

- Clarification source: https://honorcode.nd.edu/policy-update-ai-editing-tools-fall-2024

## High-school adaptation takeaways

- Define ethical use as supplementing learning rather than replacing engagement and intellectual effort.
- Cover material that is “materially modified” by AI, not only fully generated submissions.
- Explain whether AI-powered editing tools are included in an assignment’s rule.
- Require students to review outputs for bias, falsehoods, copyright issues, and proprietary information.
