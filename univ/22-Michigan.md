U-M gathers resources for faculty and students on using GenAI properly in teaching and learning. The page connects AI use to established academic integrity principles and notes reliability problems with AI-detection tools, including false positives that may unfairly penalize students.

## Academic integrity and ChatGPT

- Publishing unit: University of Michigan Library / Center for Research on Learning and Teaching
- Additional official sources:
  - https://guides.lib.umich.edu/academicintegrity
  - https://guides.lib.umich.edu/c.php?g=1039501&p=9763907

U-M defines academic misconduct as conduct that may create an unfair academic advantage for oneself or an unfair advantage or disadvantage for another member of the academic community. Upholding academic integrity is a fundamental responsibility and includes integrating other people’s ideas ethically.

The Library explains that tools such as ChatGPT, U-M GPT, and Gemini are not necessarily good or bad in themselves; they are a new way to interact with information. However:

- “ChatGPT and other generative AI tools are not search engines”; they create information and answers rather than reliably pointing to external evidence.
- Their conversational appearance is produced by mathematical models that guess likely words.
- Students therefore need to verify claims and sources instead of treating fluent language as evidence of truth.

The available university-level pages frame improper AI use through existing academic-integrity and unfair-advantage standards but do not establish one universal permission or disclosure rule for every course.

## High-school adaptation takeaways

- Define improper AI assistance partly by whether it creates an unfair academic advantage.
- Teach students that fluent AI output is generated prediction, not verified research evidence.
- Require source verification and independent checking.
- Avoid automatic penalties based only on detector output; use fair process and multiple forms of evidence.
- Allow teachers and subjects to specify more detailed rules while maintaining shared integrity principles.
