## Artificial Intelligence and the Honor Code

- Publishing unit: Emory College Learning Design
- Official source: https://online.college.emory.edu/policies/AI.html

---

Emory identifies academic integrity, ethics, access, and bias as central concerns. The page says instructors must clearly state whether AI use is permitted.

Honor Council model language for courses where AI is not allowed states:

> “Using an artificial intelligence program to generate any content for any assignment in this course (including, but not limited to examinations, papers, homework, and creative work) constitutes plagiarism and is a violation of the Honor Code.”

Using AI without instructor permission may also constitute unauthorized assistance or violate other Honor Code provisions.

Where AI is permitted, the guidance recommends that students submit the original AI-generated work and show how they edited or adapted it.

## Writing Program policy

- Additional official source: https://writingprogram.emory.edu/curriculum/GenAI-policy.html

The Emory Writing Program generally prohibits using GenAI to complete coursework, except where the instructor explicitly assigns its use for analysis, critical reflection, or a stated learning outcome.

The policy also tells students not to input instructor-created course materials—including syllabi, readings, assignment sheets, prompts, slides, or worksheets—into commercial GenAI platforms. It treats such uploading as a violation of the instructor’s intellectual-property rights and an Honor Code issue.

## High-school adaptation takeaways

- State permission or prohibition clearly in every course and assignment.
- If AI is permitted, ask students to preserve original output and explain their edits.
- Protect teacher-created materials from being uploaded to commercial systems without permission.
- Include access and bias alongside academic integrity and ethics.
