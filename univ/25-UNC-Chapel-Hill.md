## Developing AI Guidance for Faculty

- Publishing unit: AI at UNC
- Official source: https://ai.unc.edu/ai-guidance-for-faculty
- Page date: Spring 2025

---

UNC recommends four assignment-level permission categories:

1. **No AI Use:** all work is completed independently without AI assistance.
2. **Assistive AI Use Only:** AI may help with non-content-generating tasks such as grammar, formatting, or organizing ideas but may not create new intellectual content.
3. **Partial Generative AI Use:** AI may generate content or solve parts of a task, but the student must refine and modify it and use proper citations.
4. **Full Generative AI Use:** AI may generate substantial content where the assignment is designed to teach AI use; the student guides, reviews, revises, and cites the work.

### General student guidance

- “AI should help you think, not think for you.” Students must determine what is appropriate and accurate.
- Students must evaluate output for bias, limitations, inaccuracies, false output, and ethical implications.
- Personal or confidential data must not be entered into AI tools.
- “The use of AI must be open and documented.” Students should declare, explain, and cite AI use under the applicable standard.
- Students are “ultimately 100% responsible” for the final product.
- Students must follow the syllabus and ask the instructor when unsure.

The resource also encourages educators to consider accessibility, equitable participation, and AI’s environmental costs.

## High-school adaptation takeaways

- A four-level scale can distinguish proofreading from idea generation and substantial content generation.
- Make openness and documentation a condition of every permitted use.
- Students must evaluate bias, factual reliability, limitations, ethics, and confidentiality.
- Add accessibility, equitable access, and environmental impact to the school’s broader ethical framework.
