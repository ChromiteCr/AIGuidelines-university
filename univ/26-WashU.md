## Course permission and academic integrity

Students must follow the expectations established for each course and ask for clarification before using AI when the rule is uncertain. Arts & Sciences states that faculty determine whether, how, and to what extent AI may be used. The Learning Center offers a cautious practical default: when no explicit policy exists, students should not assume permission.

The central undergraduate policy treats misuse of AI as an academic-integrity issue and directs students to cite AI-generated material as they would traditionally published material, using the citation style required by the instructor. Arts & Sciences additionally calls for disclosure of whether, when, and how AI was used. Citation does not authorize a use that the course prohibits.

## Verification and responsibility

Students remain responsible for their submissions. WashU warns that AI can invent facts and citations and reproduce bias; output should be vetted, double-checked against reliable sources, and not treated as a trusted research source merely because it sounds confident. WashU IT likewise assigns users responsibility for verifying generated content.

## Privacy and intellectual property

WashU intellectual property or secure data—including deidentified healthcare data—must not be entered into a platform that has not been approved for that use. Arts & Sciences also cautions students not to upload instructor-created materials or another student’s work without explicit consent and highlights copyright, privacy, and instructor-IP concerns.

## AI detection

The reviewed central and student-facing sources do not state a university-wide position on detector reliability. Silence should not be read as approval; academic-integrity findings should rely on meaningful evidence and fair process rather than an unexplained score.

## High-school adaptation takeaways

- Publish an AI rule for every assignment; students should ask rather than assume permission.
- Require a short AI-use note naming the tool and explaining what it contributed.
- Require students to verify claims and open every cited source.
- Prohibit uploading classmates’ work, teacher materials, student records, or confidential content.
- Do not base discipline solely on an AI-detector score.
