## Guidance for Faculty & Students — Generative AI in Teaching and Learning

- Publishing unit: Office of the Provost
- Official source: https://genai.provost.virginia.edu/guidance-for-faculty-students
- Page date: Not stated

## Course permission and disclosure

Instructors may permit, limit, or prohibit AI and should make the choice explicit in syllabi and assignment directions. Students must consult the instructor when uncertain. Use that the instructor prohibits may violate the Honor Code.

Even when AI is permitted, students should identify the tool, explain how and when it was used, provide prompts, and, where appropriate, describe fact-checking. UVA advises acknowledgment of quoted, paraphrased, or incorporated AI content as well as functional uses such as editing, translation, brainstorming, and discovering sources.

## Verification and responsibility

AI can produce biased, misleading, or false claims, misattribute content, and invent sources. Students must critically assess results, verify factual claims through reliable sources, and remain responsible for submitted work.

## Privacy and intellectual property

Students should not enter sensitive, personal, or proprietary information into AI systems. External services may retain account data, prompts, and conversations or use them for training. Instructors should not require accounts that demand personal information; UVA recommends protected institutional tools when appropriate.

UVA states that students’ original work is, in most cases, their intellectual property. Instructors should not upload it to services that add submissions to training data, and an assignment requiring such an upload must offer an alternative.

## AI detection

UVA discourages AI detectors for academic-fraud decisions because reliability has not been demonstrated. Student work must not be uploaded to a detector that adds it to a database, and detector output should not be treated as proof.

## High-school adaptation takeaways

- Require teachers to publish assignment-specific AI permissions.
- Disclose meaningful assistance, including brainstorming and editing, not only copied prose.
- Verify claims and citations with textbooks, library databases, or other reliable sources.
- Offer a non-AI alternative when an activity requires account creation or upload of original work.
- Use detector results, if at all, only to prompt process review and a conversation—not as proof.
