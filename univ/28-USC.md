## Course permission and academic integrity

Students must confirm with the instructor what AI use, if any, is permitted before using it. Instructors control course-specific rules. USC’s provision of ChatGPT Edu does not change or override those rules.

AI-authored work represented as the student’s own is plagiarism whether it is copied, nearly copied, or paraphrased. USC also directs users to be transparent about where and how AI contributed to written work. Attribution does not cure use that the instructor prohibited.

## Verification and responsibility

Users must review output for accuracy, completeness, relevance, and appropriateness. USC highlights fabricated or misattributed quotations and citations as particular risks and expects human verification.

## Privacy and intellectual property

Restricted confidential or confidential data—including personally identifiable information, health information, and protected education records—must not be shared with an AI tool without appropriate consultation. Sharing includes prompts, uploads, and training data. Users remain responsible for privacy, security, retention, and intellectual-property requirements.

USC’s institutional ChatGPT Edu environment protects USC data from use in OpenAI model training, but that protection does not displace university data classifications or course rules.

## AI detection

USC states that detectors cannot definitively identify AI writing and may produce false positives. Students should be informed before detection software is used, identifying information should be removed, and detector evidence alone is insufficient to determine responsibility. Human review, contextual evidence, and preferably a conversation with the student are necessary.

## High-school adaptation takeaways

- A school-provided AI account is not blanket permission for assignments.
- Define uncredited AI-generated and AI-paraphrased work as misrepresentation.
- Require factual and citation checks plus an AI-use disclosure.
- Ban unauthorized entry of student records, health data, private communications, and class materials.
- Never discipline from a detector percentage alone; review drafts, sources, process, and explanation.
