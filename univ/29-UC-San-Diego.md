## Course permission and academic integrity

The binding policy expects individually assigned work to be completed without unauthorized aid. The AI-literacy guidance emphasizes that course expectations and the learning objectives of an assessment matter; prohibited or unauthorized aid may constitute cheating.

The reviewed campus sources do not prescribe one university-wide AI citation formula. Students must follow the instructor’s disclosure or citation requirements and should not assume that citing AI makes otherwise unauthorized assistance acceptable.

## AI literacy, verification, and responsibility

UC San Diego frames AI literacy as the ability to evaluate output origins, bias, limitations, accuracy, and broader ethical implications. Students should apply human judgment and ensure that AI supports rather than replaces the thinking or skill being assessed.

## Privacy and intellectual property

UC San Diego promotes supported platforms evaluated under UC information-security, communications, export-control, and FERPA requirements. TritonGPT is hosted on campus infrastructure so the university maintains control of its data. Because the reviewed student pages do not state one detailed universal upload rule, users must follow applicable data classifications, copyright, course rules, and platform terms and avoid uploading material they lack authority to share.

## AI detection

The AI-literacy page warns that purported AI detectors may be falsely treated as valid and accurate. The reviewed sources do not establish a detector score as reliable proof under the academic-integrity policy.

## High-school adaptation takeaways

- Ask whether AI would perform the skill being assessed and whether the teacher has explicitly allowed it.
- Preserve student ownership of the reasoning and work being assessed.
- Teach bias, hallucination, source checking, and responsible tool selection.
- Prefer school-approved platforms and prohibit confidential or unlicensed uploads.
- Treat detector scores as unreliable indicators, not findings of misconduct.
