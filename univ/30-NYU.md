## Course permission and academic integrity

NYU’s central student guidance says every course may have different generative-AI rules. Students must check the syllabus and ask the instructor before beginning; permission in one class does not carry into another.

The central student guidance says that submitting AI-generated content without acknowledgment is plagiarism and advises students to document their process when AI is permitted. Steinhardt’s narrower guidance likewise treats unattributed AI contributions or AI-generated work presented as one’s own as academic-integrity concerns and points students to NYU’s citation resources.

## Verification and responsibility

The central student guidance tells students to verify everything: AI is not a primary source, important factual claims should be corroborated using more than one credible source, and students should keep source notes. Every AI-supplied citation or link should be opened, read, and independently evaluated. Submitted work must reflect the student’s own reasoning, voice, and intellectual effort.

## Privacy and intellectual property

NYU’s licensed AI services provide stronger account-level protections than public tools; users should prefer institutionally managed services where appropriate. Steinhardt specifically warns against submitting student names, NetIDs, N-numbers, or other identifiers to unlicensed third-party services because of FERPA. Users should likewise avoid uploading copyrighted or third-party material without permission.

## High-school adaptation takeaways

- Re-check AI permission for every course and assignment.
- Require an AI-use note and, where claims rely on outside information, citations to the original sources rather than merely the chatbot.
- Open and evaluate every AI-provided link and corroborate important claims.
- Prefer school-managed accounts and never place student identifiers or records into public tools.
- Avoid detector-based enforcement; assess drafts, source notes, process evidence, and understanding.
