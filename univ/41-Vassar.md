Resources and questions to consider related to using generative AI for research

### Navigating Generative AI

This guide is designed to support Vassar community members as they navigate decisions about their use of generative AI, while recognizing that this topic, and our understanding of it, is rapidly evolving.

### Vassar AI Guidelines

Vassar College does not have an official policy on generative AI but rather empowers Vassar faculty members and departments to make tailored policies about AI for their courses;

what's permissible in one class might constitute a violation of the Academic Integrity policy in another class

.

As you make decisions about using AI for academic work:

Check the syllabus and course page on Moodle to familiarize yourself with the course AI policy.

If you have questions about it, or there isn't a policy, talk to your professor

.

If you use generative AI, make sure to disclose your use of AI.

This might look like citing it as a source, describing how you used it in the Methods section of your paper, or something else!

It's crucial that you are transparent about how you're using AI

.

Critically evaluate the outputs of AI tools

.

Verify claims; whenever possible, go to the source!

Stay informed about the ethical concerns surrounding generative AI so that you can make an informed decision about whether to use it.

### Selecting & Evaluating AI Tools

### Ithaka S+R Generative AI Product Tracker

A living document, updated regularly as new products enter the market or new information about existing products becomes available; maintained by Ithaka S+R, a non-profit organization focused on helping academic and cultural communities navigate economic, technological, and demographic change.

### AI ROBOT Test: Evaluation Tool

An evaluation tool that that prompts users to consider questions related to a tool's reliability, objective, bias, ownership, type. Developed by librarians at McGill University.

### Evaluating Sources Guide

Guidance for determining if your sources are be reliable or relevant. If you use AI tools to search for sources, verify that they are legitimate and that they are right for your research.

### Data & Privacy Considerations

Many AI platforms collect user data and chats to further train their models. Check your account settings to see if you can opt out of data collection and training.

If you are using a free account, you may have little to no privacy and data protection.

Gemini is considered a safer AI tool. As part of our campus Google suite, interactions with Gemini through your Vassar account are not shared outside of the college. Note, chats with Gemini with your Vassar account cannot be deleted.
